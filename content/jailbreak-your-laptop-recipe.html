The Ingredients

  • Omarchy — the owned OS. omarchy.org · ISO: iso.omarchy.org · github.com/omacom/omarchy
  • Bonsai — the personal AI brain (1-bit, ~1.15 GB for 8B). Runs the knowledge graph and everyday assistant. prismml.com · docs.prismml.com · huggingface.co/prism-ml/bonsai
  • Stronger coding models — for the setup agents in "Let AI Cook": Grok 4.6 (via OpenCode), GLM 5.2 / 5.1 (open weights, MIT). These are heavier than Bonsai and handle the multi-step install work.
  • llama.cpp — the runtime for Bonsai. github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp (PrismML fork: PrismML-Eng/llama.cpp)
  • Vercel — the public layer. vercel.com
  • GitHub — your source of truth. github.com
  • A domain registrar — Namecheap, Cloudflare, Porkbun, etc.
Two layers of AI: Bonsai is the lightweight personal assistant that stays on your machine forever. The stronger models (Grok 4.6, GLM 5.x) are the chefs you call in to do the heavy setup — then you can keep them local too via OpenCode + llama.cpp/Ollama, or use them only while building.

Part 1 — Jailbreak Your Laptop

Back then the lock was physical. A phone locked to one carrier, sealed by firmware, and the only way out was to crack the firmware itself — geohot soldering and coding for five hundred hours to unlock the original iPhone in 2007. Today the lock isn't the phone. It's the operating system on the laptop you use every day — the vendor's assumptions baked into the software, deciding what runs, what phones home, what you can change. Jailbreaking your laptop is the same idea: crack the lock the vendor shipped, on whatever machine you have. Mac, PC, or a Linux box you already run.

The payoff is transparency. On a locked OS you're guessing what's running, what phones home, what you can't see. On Omarchy you can list every process, every service, every connection, and know exactly what's happening because you chose it. And the AI layer turns that knowledge into control — ask your local model to audit the machine, kill a process, reconfigure a service, customize the desktop, all through conversation, all on localhost. The lock you cracked becomes a machine you can talk to.

Do this

  1. Download the ISO from iso.omarchy.org.
  2. Flash it to a USB stick (balenaEtcher on Mac/Windows).
  3. Boot off the stick. Turn off Secure Boot / TPM in BIOS.
  4. Follow the configurator — full-disk or free-space install, encryption on by default.

Part 2 — Run Your Local AI

The knowledge graph is the personal assistant that runs on your machine. Phone and laptop talk to each other over your own Wi-Fi — no account sync, no cloud relay. Bonsai 8B fits in about 1.15 GB; on a 16 GB machine it leaves room for everything else. This is the layer Bonsai was built for.

📷 Replace with: two devices hitting the same localhost assistant + server throughput log.

Do this

  1. Get a Bonsai GGUF from huggingface.co/prism-ml/bonsai (start with Bonsai-8B-Q1_0.gguf).
  2. Run it with llama.cpp: ./llama-server -m Bonsai-8B-Q1_0.gguf -c 0 --port 8080.
  3. Point your knowledge-graph app at http://localhost:8080. Expose it on your LAN IP so the phone can reach it.

Part 4 — Create Your Own Site (The Public Layer)

Claim a domain, push a repo to GitHub, let Vercel deploy on every push, then wire the domain in. This is the public face — not the brain. The brain stays local. The internet gets a site you chose.

📷 Replace with: Vercel project with green deployment + domain status "Valid Configuration".

Do this

  1. Buy a domain at your registrar.
  2. Create a GitHub repo, push your site.
  3. Import the repo into Vercel — it deploys on every push to main.
  4. In Vercel → Settings → Domains, add your domain. Set the A record (apex) or CNAME (subdomain) at your registrar.
