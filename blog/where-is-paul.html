Where is Paul?

Family asked where he was. The answer is a globe you can spin.

The texts kept coming. Sofia. Bangkok. A layover that turned into a month. By the time you named the city, he had already left it. A pin on a group chat is stale before you send it.

So the answer became a place you open. A globe. Cities as hexes. Years as a strip you can drag. Comics and photos sitting on the path, not in a camera roll you never scroll. Time as a surface.

This is the flagship of Sci-Fi Labs. A spatial web app. Not a feed. Not a photo dump. A life you move through. Same URL on a phone, a desk, and Safari on Vision Pro. Pinch to zoom. Look to look around. No store. No extra session.

https://paulvisciano.com/apps/where-is-paul/

The live map is the demo. Your copy is a clone. The only file you change is moments/moments.js.

How to read the globe

Open it. Drag the world with one finger. You are not scrolling a list of trips. You are standing over the years.

Hex color is stay. How long you actually slept there. Not how much you posted. Not how famous the city is. Height is a lie on this map. Color is the nights.

The timeline is the same life, laid flat. Cards on top. Years under them. Eras as a strip you can jump. Scrub a year and the globe turns with you. The orange pip is where you are in the story.

Pins mix media. A photo. A post. A short video. A comic drawn from real photographs. Tap one and a story opens. Mixed on purpose. A life is not one format.

If you only remember one thing: longer stay, different color. Then drag the years.

Same URL, including the headset

On a phone it is the page. On a desk it is the page. On Vision Pro it is still the page, opened in Safari. There is no separate build. Spatial apps are web apps.
