Hermetic Principles — The Keys to Mastering Your Mind

Master your mind to master the universe.

The Hermetic principles come down from a figure the Greeks called Hermes Trismegistus — “thrice-great Hermes” — a meeting of the Egyptian wisdom god Thoth and the Greek messenger Hermes. In Hellenistic and Roman Egypt, roughly 100 to 300 CE, teachers wrote the Corpus Hermeticum in his name: mind as the ground of the world, correspondence between planes, polarity. The Emerald Tablet carried the line that still travels — “as above, so below.” Those writings moved through Byzantium, then into Latin in 1471 when Marsilio Ficino translated the corpus for the Medici court. From there they fed Renaissance philosophy, alchemy, and later occultism. In 1908, in Chicago, a book signed “Three Initiates” — widely tied to the New Thought writer William Walker Atkinson — distilled that stream into seven laws and called it The Kybalion.

Seven principles. One destination: mental transmutation. You change a state instead of being dragged by it. Master the mind and the rest of the field starts to answer — not as a slogan, as a practice you can run before a talk, after a snap, on a morning that already went sideways.

The recording I am listening to is the Master Key Society audiobook — Adam Hanin reading the 1908 pages, about four hours.1 The text itself is public domain. Project Gutenberg keeps it as ebook 14209.2

THE ALL

Before the seven principles, the book names the thing they all point at: THE ALL. Not a god with a beard. Not a person. Think of a video game. The mountains, the towns, the characters — all of it exists inside the game's code. The code is not one of the things in the world. It is what the world is made of. THE ALL is that code. Not a character in the game. Not a player watching from outside. The substrate the whole thing runs on. You don't access it from outside the game. You are a character made of it, having the experience of playing.

That is why the first principle is Mentalism. If the world is held in mind, the part of it you can reach is your own mind. Start there.

Mentalism

You wake up, pick up the phone, see one message from someone who matters: “We need to talk.”

Nothing else is in the thread. No tone. No facts. Perception fills it in before your feet hit the floor — they're done, you screwed up, it's over. Heart's already going. You scroll last week for proof. By the time you reply, you're answering a conversation that hasn't happened.

The four words didn't change. Your reality did, because perception is reality for the day you actually live: what it means, what it does to you, what you do next. Another morning you read the same line as “they want time,” not “they want out.” You don't conjure a new message. You stop finishing the story before they speak.

Correspondence

“As above, so below; as below, so above.” A fractal. Look close at a fern and each small piece looks like the whole plant. A solar system and an atom both put mass in the center and orbit it.

The version that stuck for me is human. A family argument and a war between countries run on the same fuel — fear, pride, grievance. One erupts over dinner. The other, across a continent.

Because the pattern repeats, the smart move is to learn it where the stakes are low. Spot the same shape at small scale — the kitchen fight, the tense text, the awkward standup — and practice there first. Get the move right where a mistake costs little. Then carry the same move into the bigger arena, because the fuel underneath does not change with the size of the room. The scale changes. The keys do not.

Vibration

Nothing rests; everything moves; everything vibrates. A rock looks dead. It isn't. At the atomic level it is shaking — too fast and too small to see. Think of a guitar string. Pluck it hard and it hums loud. Pluck it soft and it is quiet. Same string, same note, different intensity.

Moods work the same way. You wake up with a low hum of dread — not a crisis, just a note held too long. You can fight the note, or change what is playing underneath it. A walk. A glass of water. One task you can finish in ten minutes. Each one turns the volume down a notch.

The move isn't to stop the vibration. You can't. It's to notice the rate and adjust it before it sets the tempo for the whole day. A loud morning becomes a loud afternoon if you let it. A quiet one starts the same way — one small change in the frequency.

Polarity

Opposites are one thing at different degrees. A dimmer switch. Dark and light are not enemies. They are positions on one dial. You do not invent warmth from nothing when the room is cold. You turn the knob.

Anxiety and calm sit on one dial. Same nervous system, same arousal circuit — intensity turned up or down. Say you are spiraling before a presentation. Heart racing. Worst-case loop. Telling yourself to calm down usually backfires.

Work the dial instead. Long exhale. Name the racing heart as energy, not danger. Picture one specific thing going well instead of the vague catastrophe. Each move slides you a few degrees. You do not need zero anxiety. You need to be functional. You cannot erase the pole. You can change position on it — and that is the whole game.

Rhythm

Everything swings like a pendulum. It goes out, then comes back. Markets boom, then bust. You push hard for weeks, then crash and need recovery. You cannot stop the swing. You can shorten it, or ride it instead of being caught off guard.

Moods are the easiest place to feel it. You wake up irritable, snap at someone, feel guilty, then swing back to warm and apologetic. The practical move is catching the swing early. Notice the irritability rising. Step out of the room before you say something you'll regret. Let the pendulum pass its peak instead of riding it all the way down.

Spending has the same beat — a buying spree, then a month of swearing off purchases. Plan around the return trip. Save on the high. Spend on the low. Pretending the swing will not come is how it catches you by surprise.

Cause and Effect

Nothing happens by chance. Every event has a cause, and every cause has an effect. Think of a row of dominoes. Flick the first one and the whole line falls — each domino knocks the next. Nothing in that chain is random. Every fall has a precise cause.

Life works the same way. Your habits, your choices, your reactions are the flicks that set off chains you didn't always see coming. Skip breakfast, get shaky and irritable by ten, snap at a coworker, then spend the afternoon replaying it and feeling guilty. The last domino is the apology. The first one is the skipped meal.

The practical move is finding the first domino — the skipped breakfast, the bad night's sleep, the unsent reply — and fixing that instead of apologizing for the last one. You cannot undo the chain. You can stop starting it.

Gender

Not biological sex. Two forces on every plane. Masculine: initiate, push outward, start the motion. Feminine: receive, hold, let the thing take shape. A seed without soil dies on concrete. Soil without a seed just sits there. Neither works alone.

A conversation is the clean example. One person initiates — asks the question, makes the proposal, sets the direction. The other receives it — listens, considers, responds, lets the idea take shape between them. If both only initiate, two people talk past each other. If both only receive, nothing ever starts. Creation needs both keys.

The same split shows up in a workout, a meeting, an app you are building. Someone has to throw the first idea out. Someone has to hold it and shape it. The masculine starts the motion. The feminine gives it form. Mastery is knowing which key the moment needs — and being willing to hand it over.

Mental transmutation

The seven principles are the keys. Mental transmutation is turning them. That anxiety-to-calm shift is the small version — you did not destroy the feeling. You moved along the polarity. The larger version is doing it as a habit. Catch the gear before it grinds. Choose the breath or the reframe before the spiral owns the afternoon.

I treat this the same way I treat a local stack. Knowledge Graph — the 3D canvas I run on hardware I own — is correspondence in practice: the inner archive should look like the outer life. Listening to a four-hour book is not mastery. Turning one dial is.

Master the mind first. The universe you actually live in — the talk、 the meal, the argument, the day — follows the state you hold. Which key do you reach for when the morning already went sideways?

One fight, all seven

Saturday morning. Something sharp gets said — maybe about money, maybe about nothing. They go quiet. You go quiet. Now you're both in your corners, each sure the other started it.

Mentalism. Your head is already writing the sequel — they're punishing you, they never listen, this is how it always ends. You catch the story before it runs the morning. You name it, drop it, and ask what actually happened instead of what your head says happened.

Correspondence. The same fear and pride that runs a border dispute is running this argument. You study it where it's cheap — right here, over coffee — and get the move right before it shows up somewhere it costs more.

Vibration. The anger hums. You don't fight the note. You change what's playing under it — a walk, a glass of water, one small thing you can finish. Volume down a notch.

Polarity. You don't erase the anger. You slide the dial. Long exhale, racing heart as energy, one specific thing you want instead of the vague catastrophe. Position, not zero.

Rhythm. You know the crash is coming after the high of winning the argument. You plan the repair before you need it — not after.

Cause and effect. You trace it back — the skipped breakfast, the short sleep, the thing you didn't say Tuesday. Each domino had a cause. You fix the first one, not the last.

Gender. You initiate — you say the hard thing first. Then you receive — you actually listen to what comes back, instead of waiting for your turn to talk.

None of this is theory. It's turning seven dials during one fight that matters. That is mental transmutation — not reading the map, steering by it.
