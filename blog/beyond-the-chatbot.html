Local AI still looks like a chatbot

Open WebUI, LibreChat, AnythingLLM, LobeChat, and Jan solved the hard part: run models on hardware you control. The interface is still a vertical stack of bubbles. That is not the only shape memory can take.

In 2026 the self-hosted AI stack is real. Open WebUI positions itself as a sovereign AI platform: data residency, local models, identity you administer. LibreChat ships multi-provider chat with team auth. AnythingLLM centers document workspaces and RAG. LobeChat optimizes for polish and plugins. Jan runs fully offline on the desktop. Ollama and llama.cpp handle the models underneath.

Those projects matter. They proved you do not have to send every prompt to someone else’s region. They also share one design assumption: the primary surface is a chatbot.

Self-hosting fixed the data path. It did not fix the interface. We still talk to memory through a text box and a list of messages.

What the current tools actually ship

Open WebUI is the largest community option: chat, knowledge collections, tools, multi-user controls, air-gap patterns. LibreChat is the team workbench — OIDC, LDAP, agents, MCP, many providers in one window. AnythingLLM is the document specialist: workspaces, citations, desktop or Docker. LobeChat leans into agents and a cleaner UI. Jan is the privacy-first desktop client.

All of them, at the user layer, still organize experience as conversation threads. You type. The model replies. History is a scroll. Documents become context for the next message. That pattern works. It is also the same pattern ChatGPT trained the world on.

Why a canvas is a different product

The Knowledge Graph starts from a different question: what if your photos, notes, conversations, and later video and audio were not buried inside threads, but laid out in space you can navigate?

Time becomes depth. A month is a place you fly to. A conversation is a node you can land on, not only a transcript you re-read. Retrieval is not “paste more context into the box” — it is moving the camera to the region that already holds the answer. Live conversation mode sits on top of that canvas. Multilingual support is planned as a first-class path, not an afterthought. Live audio and video are on the roadmap so the same spatial memory can hold what you said and saw, not only what you typed.

That is closer to how people already think about their own lives: places, periods, people, artifacts. It is also closer to what spatial computing and XR make possible once the UI is not glued to a phone-shaped chat column.

Sovereignty without a new chat clone

Open WebUI’s framing is useful: sovereignty is control over infrastructure, data path, models, identity, and audit — not isolation for its own sake. The same principle applies to the interface. If the only way to use a local model is still a chatbot, you have moved the server and kept the product shape of the cloud apps you were trying to leave.

Sci-Fi Labs is building the personal version of that control as a spatial stack. Knowledge Graph is the memory layer. Where is Paul? is a life map. Musical Cubes is interaction as something you turn in 3D. The published sites are demos. The product is the copy that runs on your machine.

You can pair that surface with the same local runtimes everyone else uses. The point is not to reject Open WebUI or LibreChat. It is to notice that “self-hosted” and “still a chatbot” are two different design decisions.
