Jailbreak Your Laptop

Own the OS. Own the apps. Own the stack.

The thesis

Before the iPhone was a status symbol, it was a locked box. In the summer of 2007, a seventeen-year-old named George Hotz — geohot — spent five hundred hours soldering and coding to unlock the original one. He posted the video from his parents' kitchen: "This is the world's first unlocked iPhone." He traded one of the phones for a Nissan 350Z. He was still a teenager. He didn't ask permission. He took ownership.

Back then the lock was physical — a phone sealed to one carrier by firmware, and the only way out was to crack the firmware itself. Today the lock isn't the phone. It's the operating system on the laptop you use every day: the vendor's assumptions baked into the software, deciding what runs, what phones home, what you can change. On a locked OS you can still inspect processes — the difference is what happens next. On a machine you own, something not working is a conversation. Describe it, and the local model rewrites the config. Every change visible and reversible.

This is the roadmap to sovereignty. Every layer already works.

The lock moved from the carrier to the operating system. This series cracks it — on whatever laptop you already own. Mac, PC, or a Linux box you already run. It all runs on 16 GB.
