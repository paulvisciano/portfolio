Knowledge Graph, Part 2: Voice, mobile, and the queue

Mic permissions, Metal watchdogs, conversation nodes on the canvas, and a server-side LLM job system.

Part 1 left a canvas and a pipeline. Part 2 is where the product had to survive contact with hands, microphones, and concurrent clients.

Voice is not a feature checkbox

Whisper on Metal was necessary but not sufficient. Idle Metal residency sets expire. After a few minutes of silence the next transcription hung. Health pings on CPU endpoints did not keep the GPU warm. The fix was a watchdog that forced real inference on a minimal WAV every ninety seconds — and restarted the server after consecutive failures.

On the client, recording was a weeks-long negotiation with mobile browsers. Press-and-hold, tap-to-record, double-tap for options, synthetic pointerup double-fires, iOS gesture chains broken by AudioContext.resume() before getUserMedia, HTTPS required for mic permission, crypto.randomUUID missing in non-secure contexts. Commit messages from those days read like a lab notebook: every theory tested against a phone on the local network.

HTTPS on the dev server was not polish. Without a secure context, recordingSupported stayed false and the mic never initialized on a real device.

Chat leaves the sidebar

Chat stopped being a separate panel and became an overlay on the graph — game-style, bottom-right, collapsible. Later it collapsed further into a glowing mic orb with hover options. Conversations became nodes on the canvas: portrait cards with titles, active-state glow, fly-to camera when selected. The graph was no longer only photos. It was the place conversations lived.

Then the architecture had to catch up. Direct browser calls to llama-server do not scale across devices or survive refresh. Wave by wave: job tables, claim/cancel, an event bus with SSE and pg_notify, a worker with multi-turn MCP tool loops, a client that reconnects with Last-Event-ID. Streaming indicators moved onto conversation cards. Queued responses let you record several voice notes without waiting for the model to finish.

Mobile as first-class surface

Bottom sheets, safe-area insets, 44px targets, timeline pills that did not wrap, pinch and pan that matched a reference infinite-canvas implementation, drift disabled on touch so the camera did not keep sliding after a gesture. The timeline picker evolved from a dropdown into an iOS-style wheel with continuous offset and snap-on-settle. Touch lag, boundary resets, and accidental opens were sanded down commit by commit.

By early August the system could ingest photos, answer from the graph, take voice, and stay coherent across clients — still fully local. The remaining work was less “make it work” and more “make it feel inevitable.”
