Knowledge Graph, Part 2: Voice, mobile, and the queue

Why voice needed a GPU watchdog, why chat had to live on the canvas, and the optimizations that made multi-client local AI survivable on 16GB shared memory.

Part 1 left a working canvas under a hard ceiling: the entire stack in about 16GB of shared memory. Part 2 is what happened when phones, microphones, and concurrent clients hit that budget at once — and what had to change so multi-client local AI stayed usable without abandoning the constraint.

Voice as a first-class input

Whisper on-device is only the start. Long sessions, overlapping capture, and GPU contention meant a watchdog had to own model lifecycle: load, idle timeout, and reclaim. Without it, the 16GB machine felt like a shared lab resource that could lock under load — one client’s Whisper session starving the canvas for everyone else.

Chat on the canvas

A separate chat window fought the spatial metaphor. Moving conversation onto the same surface as photos and notes kept one mental model: memory is a place you travel through, and talk is just another plane in that place.

The queue is invisible when it works — jobs for vision, speech, and graph extract land, complete, and free scarce Metal memory for the next client.

Multi-client survival

  • Job queue with clear ownership of Metal resources inside the 16GB budget
  • API paths that tolerate tablet + phone at the same time
  • Processing UI that reports progress without blocking navigation

Part 3 is where the surface became intentional: design tokens, component extraction, and better answers from the graph so the place you travel through actually earns trust — still on the same machine.
