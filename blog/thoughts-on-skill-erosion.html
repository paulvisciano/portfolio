Thoughts on Skill Erosion

The more that the machines have autonomy, the less that we have. In a way we're giving away our freedom.

I had this idea in my head. I wanted to talk it through and maybe write a blog around it.

That's a long known thing. It has to do with letting go of control.

No pedals or steering wheel

That checks. Google started the project in 2009. In October 2010 Sebastian Thrun posted that the cars had already driven from Mountain View to Santa Monica, down Lombard Street, across the Golden Gate, around Tahoe — more than 140,000 miles, a person still in the seat in case the software needed one.2 Nevada issued the first U.S. license in May 2012.3 Fifteen years ago from this week is about right.

Google even built the no-wheel version. May 2014, Chris Urmson: no steering wheel, no accelerator, no brake pedal.4 They called it Firefly. October 2015, a legally blind passenger, Steve Mahan, took a public-road trip in one with no controls and no human backup.5

Waymo's paid Jaguars still have a wheel in the car. Amazon's Zoox does not. NHTSA let Zoox charge for rides with no steering wheel and no pedals this summer.6 Tesla's Cybercab started paid rides in Austin on September 3. Small geofence. Nothing to grab. They want it to be one of the large ones. It isn't yet.1

You need to trust it in order to let go

They do know this. Lisanne Bainbridge, 1983: the more the system does, the worse you get at watching it, and the worse you are when you have to take over.8 Skill dies when you stop using it.

John Lee and Katrina See: trust in automation is the attitude that an agent will help you in a situation where you are uncertain and vulnerable.9 That is the cab with nothing to grab.

A 2022 study found people rated a fully automated car as less safe than a highly automated one even after they were shown the safety numbers. Not missing information. Perceived lack of control, then anxiety.10 No wheel reads as no way out. That's why it took decades. Not because nobody had built a car that could drive.

A basic human skill

That's the direction. It already started without robotaxis. In 1983 about half of U.S. 16-year-olds had a license. By 2022 it was about a quarter. Eighteen-year-olds went from about 80% licensed to about 60%.11 Uber, cost, cities, phones. A cab with no wheel makes not-driving the product, not a delay.

Not every car next year. If the cabs work, driving becomes optional, then rare, then a hobby. A basic human skill does not vanish in a press release. It vanishes when the next kid never needs it.

Faster than people anticipated

GPS. Not typewriters.

Dahmani and Bohbot tracked regular drivers and retested a smaller group three years later. More lifetime GPS use went with worse spatial memory when people had to navigate without it. The people who used GPS more between tests declined more. They were not using it because they already felt lost. The offload came first.12 Follow the arrow and the map in your head does less work. That's skill erosion you can measure, and it happened while everyone was still talking about self-driving as the future.

Calculators did a milder version to number sense. Phones did it to remembering a number. None of that arrived as a crisis. It arrived as a default. You notice when the default is gone and you cannot do the old job.

That's over-reliance. Turn-by-turn to a grocery store you've visited a hundred times. A rating instead of a judgment. A chatbot instead of a first draft. Use is a tool in the hand. Reliance is when the tool is the only way the task exists for you.

Now with AI

The wheel was the visible one. You can photograph its absence. AI is quieter. Writing, coding, remembering, answering. Trust, then let go, then forget you used to do it. Months, not decades.

A cab still dumps you at a curb. A model that owns the answer can shape what you believe on the way there. I wrote about that in Who owns the answer?

I take tools. I write with them. The point is to see the trade while you still have a choice. Keep a skill because you might need it, and because a life where the machine has all the autonomy is a life where you have less.
